Amino Acid Calculator

One acid at a time, or a whole chain — free mass is the molecule, residue mass is what it gives to a chain.

Each disulfide subtracts 2.016 Da.

    Residue mass = free mass − 18.015 Da (average). A chain of n residues loses n−1 waters to its bonds, then gains one back for the termini: weight = sum of residues + 18.015 Da.

    The working

      Free masses never go straight into a chain. Residues first, water once, corrections last.

      How it works

      A protein’s molecular weight is the sum of its amino-acid residue masses plus one water molecule (18.015 Da average, 18.01056 Da monoisotopic) for the two termini. Residue masses are what is left after each peptide bond loses water — which is why a residue is always 18.015 Da lighter than the free amino acid.

      That is the whole rule behind this page. Pick one amino acid above: free mass is the free molecule; residue mass is what it contributes inside a chain (free − 18.015 Da).

      Checked examples

      Insulin A chain, GIVEQCCTSICSLYQLENYCN, = 2,383.71 Da average, 2,382.00 Da monoisotopic. Ubiquitin = 8,564.84 Da. Peptide GGCK = 363.43 Da by full-precision residues (363.44 Da from a two-decimal table — see the note above). Each disulfide bond subtracts 2.016 Da.

      Average mass suits lab work and SDS-PAGE; monoisotopic mass suits high-resolution mass spectrometry.

      Traps worth knowing

      1. Free or residue? The beginner error: summing free masses overstates a chain by 18.015 Da per bond. Residues first, always.
      2. Water counted twice? One water per chain, not per residue and not per terminus. n residues, n−1 waters lost, one water back.
      3. Disulfides. Each bond subtracts 2.016 Da. A chain with cysteines does not prove a bond exists — count only the bonds you know are there.
      4. Ile against Leu. Identical mass (isomers). No mass calculator can tell them apart; the sequence must.
      5. Salt form. A TFA or HCl salt weighs more than the free peptide. Check whether the supplier’s weight includes the counter-ion.
      6. Average or monoisotopic? Picking the wrong one explains most small mismatches with a supplier’s certificate, before any other cause.
      The 18 Da error is the famous one. A result about 18 Da — or a multiple of it — away from the expected value is almost always a water-count error: free masses summed, termini added twice, or one water per residue. Count bonds: n residues, n−1 waters lost, one water back.

      Questions people actually ask

      What is the difference between free mass and residue mass?

      Free mass is the free amino-acid molecule. Residue mass is what it contributes inside a chain, after losing water when its peptide bond forms — always 18.015 Da less (average). Alanine: 89.09 Da free, 71.08 Da residue.

      Why is water subtracted at all?

      Forming a peptide bond releases H2O. A chain of n residues has n−1 bonds, so it loses (n−1) × 18.015 Da — then one water is added back for the two free termini, which is why the formula is sum of residues + 18.015.

      Average or monoisotopic — which should I use?

      Average for lab work and SDS-PAGE; monoisotopic for high-resolution mass spectrometry. The difference is small for one acid (alanine: 89.09 vs 89.048 Da) and grows with chain length.

      Why do results vary slightly between sources?

      Different sources use different precision or older atomic weight values. This site uses one canonical value per compound everywhere. Check, in order: average vs monoisotopic, cysteine state, termini, salt vs free base, and non-standard residues.

      Which amino acid is heaviest, and which is lightest?

      Tryptophan is heaviest (204.23 Da free, 186.21 Da residue); glycine is lightest (75.07 Da free, 57.05 Da residue).

      How do I calculate a peptide’s mass from this table?

      Add the residue masses, then add 18.015 Da (average) for the termini, then apply corrections: −2.016 Da per disulfide, +42.011 Da for N-acetyl, −0.984 Da for C-amide. Worked: GGCK = 363.43 Da average by full-precision residues.
