    Why the name is the hard part

    Most people know a compound by a name, not a formula. Names are looser. One name can point to several forms: copper sulfate is the anhydrous salt CuSO4 in one bottle and the pentahydrate CuSO4·5H2O in another. One formula can point to several compounds: glucose and fructose are both C6H12O6. So two questions have to be settled before any arithmetic: which compound, and which form.

    Copper sulfate has two correct answers

    • Anhydrous, CuSO4: 63.546 + 32.06 + 4 × 15.999 = 159.602 g/mol.
    • Pentahydrate, CuSO4·5H2O: 159.602 + 5 × 18.015 = 249.677 g/mol. The five waters are 90.075 g/mol, about 36% of the total.

    Weigh a pentahydrate using the anhydrous weight and you hold 159.602 ÷ 249.677 = 0.639 of the moles you think you have. Check the bottle before you calculate.

    Aspirin, resolved by name

    Aspirin is the trade name for acetylsalicylic acid, C9H8O4. Both names mean the same compound in the same form, so there is one answer: 9 × 12.011 + 8 × 1.008 + 4 × 15.999 = 180.159 g/mol. A 500 mg tablet of pure aspirin is 0.500 ÷ 180.159 = 2.78 mmol.

    Five questions before you accept a match

    1. Common, systematic or trade name? Aspirin and acetylsalicylic acid are one compound. Check the systematic name when a trade name is ambiguous.
    2. Which form — hydrate, salt, acid or base? Copper sulfate, washing soda and drug hydrochlorides all change the weight.
    3. Could the same formula be a different compound? Glucose and fructose are both 180.156 g/mol. The name separates them; the formula cannot.
    4. Is it a mixture or a polymer? Gasoline, starch, steel and milk have no single molecular weight. “No single value” is the correct answer, not a number.
    5. Element, compound or ion? Iron is 55.845; iron(III) oxide is 159.687. Roman numerals are oxidation states, not atom counts.
    Forgetting the counterion. A hydrochloride adds HCl, 36.458 g/mol, to the free base. Weighing by the base weight understates the moles of salt you actually have — roughly 15% for many drugs.

    Questions people actually ask

    Can I search by name instead of formula?

    Yes. The match, its formula and its form are shown before the number, so you can confirm it is the compound in your hand.

    What if a compound has several names?

    Aliases of one compound give one weight. Aspirin and acetylsalicylic acid are both C9H8O4 = 180.159 g/mol; baking soda and sodium bicarbonate are both NaHCO3 = 84.006 g/mol.

    Why does copper sulfate return two weights?

    The name covers the anhydrous salt at 159.602 g/mol and the pentahydrate at 249.677 g/mol. Use the form printed on your label.

    What if two compounds share a formula?

    They weigh the same. Glucose and fructose are both 180.156 g/mol. The name tells them apart; the weight cannot.

    Can I look up a brand-name drug?

    Only when the name maps to an active ingredient and a salt form. If the result does not state the salt, such as a hydrochloride, do not use it for dosing.

    What happens when the name is not found?

    You get near matches or a request for the formula. Returning the wrong compound silently is worse than returning nothing.
