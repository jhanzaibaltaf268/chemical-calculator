    The working

      Residues first, water second, corrections last. Changing the order does not change the total, but it hides mistakes.

      Residue composition

      Residues, water, corrections

      A peptide’s weight is the sum of its residue masses plus one water for the termini, adjusted for whatever chemistry the ends and the cysteines carry. The residue is the free acid minus water — 18.015 Da lighter (average).

      1. Sum the residue masses.
      2. Add one water. 18.015 Da average, 18.01056 Da monoisotopic.
      3. Apply corrections. −2.016 Da per disulfide, +42.011 Da for N-acetyl, −0.984 Da for C-amide.

      Worked by hand: GGCK

      G 57.0519 + G 57.0519 + C 103.1388 + K 128.1741 = 345.4167 Da of residues; plus 18.015 Da of water gives 363.43 Da average. A note on the last digit: some tables print this peptide as 363.44 Da, because they work from a table rounded to two decimals (free masses minus three waters: 417.49 − 54.045 = 363.445). The 0.01 Da gap is rounding in the printed table, not chemistry — full-precision residues give 363.43 Da, and this calculator uses full precision throughout.

      Traps worth knowing

      1. Free or residue? Summing free masses without subtracting water per bond overstates every peptide by 18.015 Da per residue.
      2. Disulfide sign. Forming a disulfide removes two hydrogens: subtract 2.016 Da per bond, do not add it.
      3. Termini assumed. The default is a free N-terminus and a free acid C-terminus. An acetylated or amidated peptide from a supplier is a different molecule with a different weight.
      4. Ile against Leu. Identical mass (isomers). No mass calculator can tell them apart; the sequence must.
      5. Salt form. A TFA or HCl salt weighs more than the free peptide. Check whether the supplier’s weight includes the counter-ion.
      6. Average or monoisotopic? Average for synthesis and HPLC work; monoisotopic for mass spectrometry. The gap grows with length.
      Cysteine state is the quiet one. Reduced cysteine and cystine (disulfide-bonded) differ by 2.016 Da per bond, and suppliers do not always state which form their weight assumes. Two cysteines in a sequence do not prove a bond exists — count only the bonds you know are there.

      Questions people actually ask

      How do I calculate a peptide’s mass from its sequence?

      Add the residue masses, then add one water (18.015 Da average). GGCK: 345.4167 + 18.015 = 363.43 Da.

      Why does another source say GGCK is 363.44 Da?

      Rounding in a printed table. Working from two-decimal free masses (417.49 − 54.045) gives 363.445, which prints as 363.44. Full-precision residues give 363.43. The 0.01 Da gap is the table’s rounding, not a different molecule.

      How do disulfides change the weight?

      Each disulfide bond removes two hydrogens: subtract 2.016 Da per bond from the reduced weight.

      What do N-acetyl and C-amide do to the mass?

      N-acetyl adds 42.011 Da; C-amide subtracts 0.984 Da against the free acid. Apply them only when the peptide actually carries them.

      Average or monoisotopic for a peptide?

      Average for synthesis, purification and weighing; monoisotopic for mass spectrometry, where the lightest isotope peak is what the instrument reads.
