    The working

      Residues first, water second, corrections last. Changing the order does not change the total, but it hides mistakes.

      Residue composition

      Residues, water, corrections

      A peptide’s weight is the sum of its residue masses plus one water for the termini, adjusted for whatever chemistry the ends and the cysteines carry. Longer chains use the same rule — the Protein Molecular Weight calculator applies it to full proteins. The residue is the free acid minus water — 18.015 Da lighter (average). The Amino Acid calculator shows free mass against residue mass for one acid at a time.

      1. Sum the residue masses.
      2. Add one water. 18.015 Da average, 18.01056 Da monoisotopic.
      3. Apply corrections. −2.016 Da per disulfide, +42.011 Da for N-acetyl, −0.984 Da for C-amide.

      Worked by hand: GGCK

      G 57.0519 + G 57.0519 + C 103.1388 + K 128.1741 = 345.4167 Da of residues; plus 18.015 Da of water gives 363.43 Da average. A note on the last digit: some tables print this peptide as 363.44 Da, because they work from a table rounded to two decimals (free masses minus three waters: 417.49 − 54.045 = 363.445). The 0.01 Da gap is rounding in the printed table, not chemistry — full-precision residues give 363.43 Da, and this calculator uses full precision throughout.

      Worked by hand: CGCK with one disulfide

      C 103.1388 + G 57.0519 + C 103.1388 + K 128.1741 = 391.5036 Da of residues; plus 18.015 Da of water gives 409.5186 Da for the reduced peptide. The two cysteines can form one disulfide, so subtract 2.016 Da: 409.5186 − 2.016 = 407.50 Da average. The reduced and oxidised forms differ by exactly that 2.016 Da — which is why the bond count, not just the cysteine count, has to be stated.

      Worked by hand: Ac-ACDE-NH2 (N-acetyl and C-amide)

      A 71.0788 + C 103.1388 + D 115.0886 + E 129.1155 = 418.4217 Da of residues; plus 18.015 Da of water gives 436.4367 Da, or 436.44 Da, for the free peptide. N-acetyl adds 42.011 Da and C-amide subtracts 0.984 Da, a net +41.027 Da: 436.4367 + 42.011 − 0.984 = 477.4637, so 477.46 Da average. The two end corrections together move the weight by 41.027 Da, not by either correction alone.

      Which termini does your peptide actually have?

      The default on this page is a free N-terminus and a free acid C-terminus, which is what a biological peptide carries. A synthetic peptide from a supplier very often carries the other pair: acetylated N-terminus and amidated C-terminus, added for stability. The two pairs differ by 41.027 Da, so a supplier peptide calculated as free/free comes out 41.027 Da too light. Before trusting any peptide weight, read the certificate or the synthesis specification for the two ends, and set the two boxes to match. ACDE free is 436.44 Da; the same sequence as Ac-ACDE-NH₂ is 477.46 Da — same residues, different molecule.

      All corrections at once: one peptide, four adjustments

      Take CGCK with one disulfide, an acetylated N-terminus and an amidated C-terminus, and stack every correction in order. Residues: C 103.1388 + G 57.0519 + C 103.1388 + K 128.1741 = 391.5036 Da.       What the sequence sum still does not include

      Three things sit outside the sequence entirely. First, the salt form: a TFA or HCl salt weighs more than the free peptide by the counterion mass, which no residue sum ever sees — check whether the supplier’s weight is salt or free before comparing. Second, purity against content: purity (by HPLC) is the target peptide’s share among peptide impurities, while content (by amino acid analysis or UV) is the peptide’s share of the weighed powder against salts and moisture. A sample at 95% purity but 80% content holds 0.80 mg of peptide per weighed milligram, not 0.95 mg — and it is content, not purity, that sets how much peptide a weighed amount actually holds. Third, the descriptive extras other tools report from the same sequence — the molecular formula, the hydropathy plot, GRAVY (the grand average of hydropathy) and solubility predictions — describe the peptide’s character; none of them changes its weight.

      2. Your supplier’s peptide is Ac-…-NH₂, but you calculated it as free/free. How far off is the result, and in which direction?
      3. A sequence has 3 cysteines. How many disulfides can it form at most?

      Traps worth knowing

      1. Free or residue? Summing free masses without subtracting water per bond overstates every peptide by 18.015 Da per residue.
      2. Disulfide sign. Forming a disulfide removes two hydrogens: subtract 2.016 Da per bond, do not add it.
      3. Termini assumed. The default is a free N-terminus and a free acid C-terminus. An acetylated or amidated peptide from a supplier is a different molecule with a different weight.
      4. Ile against Leu. Identical mass (isomers). No mass calculator can tell them apart; the sequence must.
      5. Salt form. A TFA or HCl salt weighs more than the free peptide. Check whether the supplier’s weight includes the counter-ion.
      6. Average or monoisotopic? Average for synthesis and HPLC work; monoisotopic for mass spectrometry. The gap grows with length.
      7. Non-standard letters skipped. B, J, O, U, X and Z are not among the 20 standard codes, so this calculator skips and reports them rather than counting them — check the sequence before trusting a weight built from it.
      8. Odd cysteine count. Three cysteines can form at most one disulfide, with one thiol left free. Entering two bonds over-subtracts 2.016 Da.
      9. One end corrected, the other forgotten. Supplier peptides usually carry both modifications. Applying only the acetyl leaves the result 0.984 Da too heavy; applying only the amide leaves it 42.011 Da too light. Set both boxes or neither, as the certificate says.
      10. Purity weighed as content. Weighing 1 mg of a 95%-pure, 80%-content sample gives 0.80 mg of peptide, not 0.95 mg. Content, not purity, sets how much peptide a weighed amount holds.
      Cysteine state is the quiet one. Reduced cysteine and cystine (disulfide-bonded) differ by 2.016 Da per bond, and suppliers do not always state which form their weight assumes. Two cysteines in a sequence do not prove a bond exists — count only the bonds you know are there.

      Questions people actually ask

      How do I calculate a peptide’s mass from its sequence?

      Add the residue masses, then add one water (18.015 Da average). GGCK: 345.4167 + 18.015 = 363.43 Da.

      Why does another source say GGCK is 363.44 Da?

      Rounding in a printed table. Working from two-decimal free masses (417.49 − 54.045) gives 363.445, which prints as 363.44. Full-precision residues give 363.43. The 0.01 Da gap is the table’s rounding, not a different molecule.

      How do disulfides change the weight?

      Each disulfide bond removes two hydrogens: subtract 2.016 Da per bond from the reduced weight.

      What do N-acetyl and C-amide do to the mass?

      N-acetyl adds 42.011 Da; C-amide subtracts 0.984 Da against the free acid. Apply them only when the peptide actually carries them.

      Average or monoisotopic for a peptide?

      Average for synthesis, purification and weighing; monoisotopic for mass spectrometry, where the lightest isotope peak is what the instrument reads.

      Why is my peptide heavier on the supplier’s certificate than here?

      Check in order, before disputing the value: salt form against free peptide, average against monoisotopic, disulfides counted or not, and N-acetyl or C-amide present or not. Compare like with like on all four, and most gaps disappear.

      Can a mass calculation tell isoleucine from leucine?

      No. Ile and Leu are isomers with the same average residue mass, 113.1594 Da, so any sequence swapping one for the other weighs exactly the same. Only the sequence itself can tell them apart.

      What happens if my sequence contains X, B or another unusual letter?

      Only the 20 standard one-letter codes count. Unusual letters are skipped and reported, so the weight is for the counted residues only — confirm what that letter was meant to stand for before weighing anything out.

      What do N-acetyl and C-amide together do to the mass?

      They add a net +41.027 Da: +42.011 Da for N-acetyl and −0.984 Da for C-amide. For ACDE that moves the free peptide from 436.44 Da to 477.46 Da.

      How do I know which termini my peptide has?

      Read the supplier’s certificate or the synthesis specification. Biological peptides are free/free; synthetic peptides are very often acetyl/amide. The two pairs differ by 41.027 Da, so guessing wrong moves the weight by exactly that much.

      What is the difference between peptide purity and peptide content?

      Purity (by HPLC) is the target peptide’s share among peptide impurities; content (by amino acid analysis or UV) is the peptide’s share of the weighed powder against salts and moisture. A 95%-pure, 80%-content sample holds 0.80 mg of peptide per weighed milligram.

      Do I need the molecular formula too?

      Not for the weight — the residue sum already gives it. The formula is a cross-check on composition, and some tools report it alongside the mass, but it feeds nothing into the calculation.

      What is GRAVY?

      The grand average of hydropathy: the mean hydrophobicity score of the residues. It describes solubility character — positive means more hydrophobic — but it does not change the weight.

      Can this calculator tell me the pI or net charge?

      No — this page does weight only. pI and net charge need pKa values for every ionizable group (Lehninger 1982 values are the usual set) and a charge-vs-pH calculation, which is what the dedicated peptide-property tools do.
