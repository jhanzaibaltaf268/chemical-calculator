    The working

      Count first, then add. The water line is the one most people miss — or add twice.

      Residue composition

      Residues, plus one water

      A protein’s weight is the sum of its amino-acid residue masses plus one water molecule for the two termini. A residue is what is left after its peptide bond loses water — always 18.015 Da lighter than the free acid (average). The Amino Acid calculator shows free mass against residue mass for one acid at a time.

      1. Count the residues. One letter each; the count per acid is the composition table below the calculator.
      2. Multiply by residue masses and add.
      3. Add one water. 18.015 Da average, 18.01056 Da monoisotopic. This is the termini, not an extra residue.

      Worked by hand: ubiquitin, 76 residues

      Ubiquitin’s sequence sums to 8,546.83 Da of residues; plus 18.015 Da of water gives 8,564.84 Da average (8.565 kDa). Summing the free acids instead would give 9,915.97 Da — overstated by 75 × 18.015 Da, one water per bond instead of one per chain.

      Insulin’s A chain, GIVEQCCTSICSLYQLENYCN (21 residues), comes to 2,383.71 Da average and 2,382.00 Da monoisotopic by the same rule.

      Worked by hand: a short chain, GGCK, residue by residue

      GGCK has four residues. Average masses: Gly 57.0519 + Gly 57.0519 + Cys 103.1388 + Lys 128.1741 = 345.4167 Da of residues; plus one water, 18.015 Da, gives 363.43 Da average. Monoisotopic masses: Gly 57.02146 + Gly 57.02146 + Cys 103.00919 + Lys 128.09496 = 345.14707 Da; plus 18.01056 Da of water gives 363.16 Da monoisotopic. The one water is added once, at the end, however long the chain is.

      Worked by hand: mature ubiquitin, and one disulfide

      Removing ubiquitin’s starting methionine removes one residue, 131.1925 Da average: 8,564.84 − 131.19 = 8,433.65 Da average for the mature chain (8,428.58 Da monoisotopic). Each disulfide then subtracts a further 2.016 Da. The two-cysteine chain CC is 224.2926 Da average reduced (103.1388 + 103.1388 + 18.015); with its one disulfide it is 224.2926 − 2.016 = 222.2766 Da, i.e. 222.28 Da average.

      Which mass do I report?

      Average for bulk work, monoisotopic for mass spectrometry — and the choice matters more as the chain grows. Average mass suits SDS-PAGE, gel filtration, weighing and buffers, because those measure the whole isotope mixture. Monoisotopic mass suits high-resolution mass spectrometry of peptides, because the instrument resolves the lightest-isotope peak. The gap between the two grows with size, because the isotope envelope widens: insulin A (21 residues) differs by 1.71 Da, ubiquitin (76 residues) by 5.23 Da, and for a large protein the monoisotopic peak becomes a small share of the total, which is why big proteins are quoted in average mass even in mass spectrometry.

      Insulin A, 21 residues — average minus monoisotopic = 1.71 Da

      Ubiquitin, 76 residues — average minus monoisotopic = 5.23 Da

      Same rule, longer chain, wider gap. Bars are scaled to ubiquitin.

      From absorbance to concentration: ε280

      Three things absorb at 280 nm: tryptophan, tyrosine, and disulfide-bonded cysteines. The Pace et al. (1995) values give ε280 = 5,500 × Trp + 1,490 × Tyr + 125 × cystine, in M⁻¹cm⁻¹. Insulin A has 2 tyrosines and 2 cystines (its 4 cysteines in 2 disulfides): 2 × 1,490 + 2 × 125 = 3,230 M⁻¹cm⁻¹. Ubiquitin has 1 tyrosine, no tryptophan and no disulfides: 1,490 M⁻¹cm⁻¹. Note the disulfide state: reduced cysteines contribute nothing, so the same sequence gives a different ε280 reduced and oxidized. Then Beer–Lambert turns an absorbance into a concentration: c = A280 ÷ (ε × l). A protein with ε = 43,824 reading A280 = 0.55 in a 1 cm cuvette is 0.55 ÷ 43,824 = 12.6 µM. Once you have ε280, the Protein Molarity calculator converts between mg/mL and µM.

      What the sequence sum does not include

      The sum is the unmodified chain, and real proteins often weigh more. A single phosphate adds about 80 Da; sugar chains on glycoproteins add up to tens of kilodaltons; a signal peptide removed after translation subtracts its residues; a fusion tag adds its own (a His-tag alone is 822.85 Da). If the measured mass beats the calculated one by a round modification-sized step, the sequence is probably right and the modification is missing. Two things other tools report from the same sequence — elemental composition and hydrophobic percentage — describe the chain but do not change its weight. And when no sequence is at hand at all, the rough estimate is about 110 Da per residue: 250 residues is roughly 27,500 Da, or 27.5 kDa.

      Answers: (1) 250 × 110 = 27,500 Da, about 27.5 kDa. (2) 3 × 2.016 = 6.048 Da lighter. (3) 5,000.00 + 18.015 = 5,018.02 Da average.

      Traps worth knowing

      1. Free or residue? Summing free masses without subtracting water per bond overstates every protein by 18.015 Da per residue. The classic beginner error.
      2. Water counted twice? One water per chain, not per residue and not per terminus. n residues, n−1 bonds, one water back.
      3. Starting methionine. Many mature proteins lose it. Keeping it adds 131.19 Da average to a weight that should not have it.
      4. Tags and fusions. A His-tag is small; a GFP fusion adds about 27 kDa. The sequence you type must be the construct you weigh.
      5. Glycosylation. Sequence sums understate glycoproteins. Use the measured value when the assay counts whole molecules.
      6. Average or monoisotopic? Average for lab work and SDS-PAGE; monoisotopic for high-resolution mass spectrometry. The gap grows with chain length.
      7. Disulfides. Each disulfide bond subtracts 2.016 Da. This page sums the reduced chain; the Peptide calculator applies the correction.
      8. Modified termini. This page assumes free termini. An acetylated N-terminus adds about 42.01 Da and an amidated C-terminus subtracts about 0.98 Da, so state the termini before comparing with a measured mass.
      9. Monoisotopic mass for a large protein. For a large protein the monoisotopic peak is a tiny share of the isotope envelope, and many instruments cannot resolve it. Quote average mass for large proteins even when the question says mass spectrometry.
      10. Letters outside the 20 codes. This page skips unknown letters and reports them, never counting them. A typo in the sequence silently shortens the chain unless you read that report — other tools average ambiguous codes instead, so the same pasted sequence can give different totals elsewhere.
      The 18 Da error is the famous one. A result about 18 Da — or a multiple of it — away from the expected value is almost always a water-count error: free masses summed, termini added twice, or one water per residue. Count bonds: n residues, n−1 waters lost, one water back.

      Questions people actually ask

      How do I calculate a protein’s weight from its sequence?

      Add the residue masses, then add one water (18.015 Da average). Ubiquitin: 8,546.83 + 18.015 = 8,564.84 Da.

      Should I use average or monoisotopic mass?

      Average for lab work and SDS-PAGE; monoisotopic for high-resolution mass spectrometry. Insulin A is 2,383.71 Da average and 2,382.00 Da monoisotopic.

      Why is my result about 18 Da too high?

      Almost always a water-count error — free masses summed without subtracting per bond, or termini added twice. Count bonds: n residues, n−1 waters lost.

      Do I remove the starting methionine?

      For a mature protein, usually yes — many lose it after translation. The calculator offers the choice because the sequence as written and the protein as weighed often differ by exactly that residue.

      Why do isoleucine and leucine have the same mass?

      Same formula (C6H13NO2), different arrangement — isomers. No mass calculator can distinguish them; you need sequence information.

      How do I audit a result in seconds?

      Add the residue masses, then add one water: 18.015 Da average, 18.01056 Da monoisotopic. If that sum does not match the total, a residue or a terminus is wrong. For GGCK: 345.4167 + 18.015 = 363.43 Da average.

      How much does one disulfide change the weight?

      Each disulfide subtracts 2.016 Da. The two-cysteine chain CC is 224.29 Da average reduced and 222.28 Da with its one disulfide. This page sums the reduced chain.

      What if my protein has two chains or subunits?

      Weigh each chain separately, because each chain gets its own water: one water per chain, not one per protein. Add the chain weights together afterwards, then apply any disulfides that join the chains.

      Does a His-tag change the weight by a fixed amount?

      Six histidine residues alone add 822.85 Da average (6 × 137.1411 Da), with no extra water, since the chain still has only two termini. Any linker or cleavage site adds more, so type the full construct sequence, not just the protein part.

      What is the extinction coefficient, and how do I use it?

      ε280 = 5,500 × Trp + 1,490 × Tyr + 125 × cystine (Pace et al. 1995). Then Beer–Lambert: c = A280 ÷ (ε × l). A protein with ε = 43,824 reading A280 = 0.55 in a 1 cm cuvette is 12.6 µM.

      Why does the average–monoisotopic gap grow with size?

      The isotope envelope widens as the molecule grows, so the average (whole mixture) drifts further from the lightest-isotope peak. Insulin A differs by 1.71 Da; ubiquitin by 5.23 Da; a large protein by far more.

      Can I estimate the weight without a sequence?

      Roughly: about 110 Da per residue. 250 residues is roughly 27,500 Da, or 27.5 kDa. Some tools accept a residue count alone for exactly this estimate, but it never replaces the sequence sum.

      Can I paste a FASTA file?

      Paste the sequence only, not the header line. This page strips spaces and line breaks, but header letters would be read as residues — a header like “ubiquitin” would add I, Q, T and N to the chain. Copy from the first residue letter.

      How do I convert Da to kDa?

      Divide by 1,000. Ubiquitin at 8,564.84 Da is 8.565 kDa. One dalton equals 1 g/mol, so the same number serves as the molar mass.
