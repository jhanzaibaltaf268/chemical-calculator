RNA Calculator

RNA weight from the bases — monophosphate residues summed, waters removed, 5′ end stated.

T is read as U. Unknown letters are skipped and reported, never silently counted.

The 5′ end is the biggest single swing in an RNA weight. State it, every time.

Try:
—

    Residue monophosphates: A 329.21, U 306.17, G 345.22, C 305.18 Da. Sum them, subtract 18.015 Da per bond, then adjust the 5′ end.

    The working

      Residues first, waters second, end last. The end adjustment is a statement about the molecule, not a rounding choice.

      Base composition

      Residues, waters, end

      An RNA weight is the sum of its nucleotide residue monophosphates minus one water per phosphodiester bond, adjusted for the 5′ end. The residue values already carry one phosphate each, so the chain’s weight is exact for its stated ends — unlike a length estimate.

      1. Sum the residues. A 329.21, U 306.17, G 345.22, C 305.18 Da, plus 329.21 Da per poly-A residue.
      2. Subtract the waters. (length − 1) × 18.015 Da, one per bond.
      3. Adjust the 5′ end. Phosphate is the basis (no change); 5′-OH subtracts 79.98 Da; 5′-triphosphate adds about 159 Da.

      Worked by hand: AUG, 5′-phosphate

      A 329.21 + U 306.17 + G 345.22 = 980.60 Da of residues; minus 2 × 18.015 = 36.03 Da of waters gives 944.57 Da. The same sequence as 5′-OH is 944.57 − 79.98 = 864.59 Da — the end alone moves the answer by 80 Da, which is why it is never left unstated.

      Worked by hand: AUG, 5′-OH

      The residues and waters are unchanged: A 329.21 + U 306.17 + G 345.22 = 980.60 Da, minus 2 × 18.015 = 36.03 Da, giving 944.57 Da on the phosphate basis. Then apply the end: 944.57 − 79.98 = 864.59 Da. Same three bases, same two bonds — only the 5′ end differs, and the answer drops by 79.98 Da.

      Worked by hand: AUG + 10 A tail, 5′-phosphate

      Residues: 980.60 Da for AUG plus 10 × 329.21 = 3,292.10 Da for the tail, giving 4,272.70 Da. The chain is now 13 bases, so waters are 12 × 18.015 = 216.18 Da. Total: 4,272.70 − 216.18 = 4,056.52 Da (4.057 kDa). Cross-check from the tail-free weight: 944.57 + 3,111.95 = 4,056.52 Da, where 3,111.95 Da is 10 × (329.21 − 18.015).

      Why RNA residues are heavier than DNA residues

      Each RNA residue carries one more oxygen than its DNA cousin: the 2′-OH on the ribose sugar. The numbers show it: A 329.21 vs 313.2 Da (16.01 Da more), G 345.22 vs 329.2 Da (16.02 Da more), C 305.18 vs 289.2 Da (15.98 Da more) — each about one oxygen (15.999 Da), the small gaps being table rounding. U against T is the exception that proves the rule: uracil’s residue is 306.17 Da, and thymine is uracil plus a methyl group (CH₂, 14.027 Da) minus that same oxygen, 306.17 − 15.999 + 14.027 = 304.20 Da — exactly DNA’s T residue. DNA follows the same residues-minus-waters pattern with T in place of U — see the DNA calculator.

      The three 5′ ends, side by side

      The same three bases, AUG, in all three 5′ forms. The extremes differ by 159 + 79.98 = 238.98 Da, about 239 Da — more than a whole extra base, from the end alone. Bars are scaled to the triphosphate form.

      AUG, 5′-OH — 864.59 Da

      AUG, 5′-phosphate — 944.57 Da

      AUG, 5′-triphosphate — 1,103.57 Da

      Same sequence, same length, three weights. State the end before quoting the number.

      Two conventions, one answer — do not mix them

      This page sums monophosphate residues (A 329.21 Da and friends) and then subtracts one water per bond. Other tables list residues already net of one water each (329.21 − 18.015 = 311.20 Da for A) and pair them with different end terms. Both routes reach the same chain weight when each table is used with its own end terms. Mixing them is the error: subtracting waters from already-netted residues double-counts the water, and forgetting waters from monophosphates misses it entirely. If a source’s arithmetic looks 18 Da per bond away from yours, check which convention its table uses before assuming a counting error.

      The tail takes over

      A poly-A tail quickly outweighs the message it follows. AUG alone is 944.57 Da; with 10 extra A residues it is 4,056.52 Da; with 100 it is 32,064.07 Da. Bars are scaled to the 100-tail weight.

      AUG, no tail — 944.57 Da

      AUG + 10 A — 4,056.52 Da

      AUG + 100 A — 32,064.07 Da

      Each added A contributes 311.20 Da after its bond water; a long tail is most of the molecule.

      From weight to moles and copies

      The weight turns a weighed amount into moles and molecules. Picomoles = nanograms × 1,000 ÷ weight in Da. Worked for AUG + 10 A tail (4,056.52 Da): one microgram is 1,000,000 ÷ 4,056.52 = 246.5 pmol, and 246.5 pmol × 6.02214076 × 10₂³ ÷ 10¹₂ = 1.48 × 10¹⁴ copies. The Molarity calculator turns moles and volume into molarity for any solute.

      Try it yourself

      1. AUG as 5′-phosphate is 944.57 Da. What is it as 5′-triphosphate on this page’s basis, and on the 178 Da basis?
      2. How much does one microgram of AUG + 10 A tail (4,056.52 Da) contain, in picomoles?
      3. A 1,000-base mRNA averages 321.445 Da per residue. Roughly how much does it weigh?

      Answers: (1) 944.57 + 159 = 1,103.57 Da here; 944.57 + 178 = 1,122.57 Da on the other basis. (2) 1,000,000 ÷ 4,056.52 = 246.5 pmol. (3) 1,000 × 321.445 − 999 × 18.015 = 303,448 Da, about 303 kDa (before the 5′ end adjustment).

      Sources: ThermoFisher residue weights; this page’s 5′ end terms (phosphate basis, OH −79.98, triphosphate +159).

      Traps worth knowing

      1. U or T? RNA uses uracil (306.17 Da residue); DNA uses thymine. Typing a DNA sequence into an RNA calculator understates every T position.
      2. The 5′ end unstated. Phosphate, hydroxyl and triphosphate differ by up to about 239 Da between the extremes. A weight without its end basis is not a complete answer.
      3. Poly-A tail counts. Each added A is a full 329.21 Da residue minus its bond water. Ten extra A residues add about 3,112 Da, not a round number.
      4. Length rules are estimates. A per-base average (about 330–340 Da) is fine for a gel; for a synthesised oligo, sum the actual bases.
      5. Modified bases. Pseudouridine, methylated bases and caps are not in the four-letter table. Add their residue masses explicitly.
      6. Forgetting the waters. Summing residues alone overstates the weight by (length − 1) × 18.015 Da — for a 100-base chain, 99 × 18.015 = 1,783.49 Da too high. Subtract the waters before quoting the number.
      7. Double-counting the tail. If the tail A residues are already in the pasted sequence, do not also enter them in the poly-A field, or they are counted twice. Put the tail in one place only.
      8. Triphosphate corrections differ by source. This page adds 159 Da for a 5′-triphosphate; some sources add 178 Da, a 19 Da gap. Compare triphosphate weights only on the same stated basis.
      9. Mixing residue tables with end terms. This page’s residues are monophosphates with waters subtracted afterwards; other tables net the water into the residue and use different end terms. Pairing one table with the other’s end term shifts the answer by 18 Da per bond.
      10. A cap is not a triphosphate. A capped mRNA carries a methylated guanosine cap at the 5′ end, a different group from a triphosphate. Using the +159 Da triphosphate term for a capped transcript understates the cap’s mass — state the cap explicitly instead.
      The end is the expensive one. Base-counting errors move the answer by hundreds of daltons per base, but the 5′ end moves it silently — same sequence, same length, three different weights. State the end before quoting the number.

      Questions people actually ask

      How do I calculate RNA weight from a sequence?

      Sum the residue monophosphates (A 329.21, U 306.17, G 345.22, C 305.18), subtract 18.015 Da per bond, then adjust the 5′ end. AUG as 5′-phosphate is 944.57 Da.

      What does the 5′ end change?

      Phosphate is the basis. 5′-OH subtracts 79.98 Da; 5′-triphosphate adds about 159 Da. AUG is 944.57 Da as phosphate and 864.59 Da as hydroxyl.

      How does a poly-A tail change the weight?

      Each added A contributes 329.21 Da minus one bond water (18.015 Da), so about 311.20 Da per residue. Ten extra A residues add about 3,112 Da.

      Can I use DNA weights for RNA?

      No. RNA residues carry an extra oxygen (the 2′-OH) and uracil replaces thymine. A DNA weight for the same letters is a different molecule.

      Why does another source give a different RNA weight?

      Usually the 5′ end basis, the poly-A tail, or a length-average rule instead of a base sum. Check all three before assuming either is wrong.

      Why do two sources differ by 19 Da for a 5′-triphosphate RNA?

      They use different triphosphate corrections: this page adds 159 Da, some sources add 178 Da. AUG is 944.57 + 159 = 1,103.57 Da here, against 944.57 + 178 = 1,122.57 Da on the other basis — neither is a counting error, they are different stated bases.

      How do I convert an RNA weight from Da to kDa?

      Divide by 1,000. AUG + 10 A tail at 4,056.52 Da is 4.057 kDa, and AUG alone at 944.57 Da is 0.945 kDa.

      What happens if I paste T, lowercase letters, or unknown letters?

      T is read as U, lowercase is uppercased, and unknown letters are skipped and reported, never silently counted. Pasting a DNA-style sequence therefore gives an RNA weight for the U-substituted letters — check the reported skips before trusting the result.

      How heavy should a tRNA be, as a sanity check?

      Roughly 25–30 kDa. If your result for a tRNA-sized RNA lands in the hundreds of daltons or the hundreds of kilodaltons, recheck the sequence length, the poly-A tail, and the 5′ end before anything else.

      Why is RNA heavier than DNA for the same letters?

      The 2′-OH oxygen: each RNA residue carries one more oxygen (15.999 Da) than its DNA cousin. A is 329.21 vs 313.2 Da; G is 345.22 vs 329.2 Da; C is 305.18 vs 289.2 Da. U against T nets the other way, because thymine adds a methyl group on top: 306.17 − 15.999 + 14.027 = 304.20 Da.

      What is the difference between a 5′ cap and a 5′ triphosphate?

      A triphosphate is three phosphates in a row (+159 Da here); a cap is a methylated guanosine joined through its own triphosphate bridge, a much larger group. Do not use the triphosphate term for a capped transcript — state the cap explicitly.

      How many copies are in a microgram?

      Picomoles = nanograms × 1,000 ÷ weight in Da, then multiply by Avogadro’s number. One microgram of AUG + 10 A tail (4,056.52 Da) is 246.5 pmol, or 1.48 × 10¹⁴ copies.

      How heavy should an mRNA be, as a sanity check?

      Roughly 300 kDa per 1,000 bases: 1,000 × 321.445 − 999 × 18.015 = 303,448 Da, before the 5′ end adjustment. A tRNA-sized RNA is roughly 25–30 kDa. If your result lands far outside those bands, recheck the length, the tail, and the end.

      Does this page handle double-stranded RNA?

      No — it treats RNA as single-stranded. A double-stranded RNA needs both strands summed separately, because the complement carries different residues, exactly as the DNA calculator explains for double-stranded DNA.
